# AdaptED Pitch Deck

**Live demo:** https://adapted-fawn.vercel.app/  
**Presentable deck:** https://adapted-fawn.vercel.app/pitch (or `/pitch` locally)

Use this doc as speaker-notes reference. The interactive deck at `/pitch` is the primary presentation — arrow keys to navigate, **N** for notes, **F** for fullscreen, print to PDF from the browser. Each slide has a **headline** (big on screen), **on-slide text** (minimal), **visual** (what to show), and **speaker notes** (what you say).

---

## Why most AdaptED decks fall flat

| Mistake | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Opens with "AdaptED is an AI EdTech platform…" | Open with the problem or a before/after transformation |
| Lists four modes as bullet points | Show one assignment transforming live — modes are proof, not the pitch |
| Sounds like every accessibility startup | Lead with **structural** adaptation, not "simpler words" |
| 40 words per slide | One idea per slide; headline does the work |
| No demo moment | Slide 4–5 should be the website, not a diagram |
| Generic stats with no source | Use the stats below — cite them verbally if asked |
| Mismatched modes (visual/hearing vs ADHD/APD) | Product ships **Dyslexia, ADHD, APD, Autism** — stay consistent |
| Ends with "thank you" | End with a clear ask: try the demo, vote, pilot, or invest |

## Version A — Hackathon / demo day (7 slides · ~3 minutes)

Best when judges will see you live. Optimize for **one memorable moment**: same assignment, four structurally different outputs in under 30 seconds.

---

### Slide 1 — Title

**Headline:** AdaptED  
**Subhead:** One lesson. Every learner.  
**On slide:** adapted-fawn.vercel.app

**Visual:** Clean dark slide. Logo "A" badge. No paragraphs.

**Speaker notes:**  
"AdaptED helps teachers take one assignment and instantly adapt it for students who learn differently — dyslexia, ADHD, auditory processing, and autism. I'll show you in 30 seconds."

---

### Slide 2 — The problem (hook)

**Headline:** Education asks students to adapt — not the other way around.

**On slide (3 short lines):**
- 1 in 5 students face learning accessibility barriers
- Teachers spend 7+ hours/week rewriting materials
- The assignment stays rigid; the student is expected to change

**Visual:** Single classroom photo or simple icon row. No wall of text.

**Speaker notes:**  
"Every teacher has sent home the same worksheet to 30 kids. But one in five processes information differently. The fix today is manual — teachers stay up late simplifying, chunking, reformatting. That's 7+ hours a week, and it's never consistent. We think the assignment should adapt to the learner, not the reverse."

---

### Slide 3 — The insight

**Headline:** Same lesson. Four learning experiences.

**On slide:**  
Accessibility isn't one setting. It's **structure** — how content is chunked, sequenced, and organized.

**Visual:** Four small cards in a row: Dyslexia · ADHD · APD · Autism (use brand colors from the site).

**Speaker notes:**  
"A student with dyslexia needs short chunks and simpler scanning. A student with ADHD needs one clear step at a time. Auditory processing needs written structure from spoken-style content. Autism needs predictable sections with zero ambiguity. These aren't four synonyms — they're four different document structures."

---

### Slide 4 — The product (demo slide) ★ most important

**Headline:** Paste once. Adapt instantly.

**On slide:**  
Upload · Pick mode · Share the version that fits each learner  
**&lt; 30 seconds · No sign-up**

**Visual:** Screenshot or screen recording of the live demo — split view with photosynthesis assignment, Dyslexia mode output visible. **Better: switch to the live site here.**

**Speaker notes:**  
"This is AdaptED. I'm pasting a real assignment about photosynthesis. Watch what happens when I switch modes…"  
*[Live demo: Dyslexia → ADHD → APD → Autism. Pause 2 seconds on each so the structure difference is obvious.]*  
"Same content. Four completely different reading experiences. Under 30 seconds."

### Slide 4b — Side-by-side proof (optional backup slide)

Use if you can't demo live (bad Wi‑Fi, projector issues).

**Headline:** Not a rewrite. A restructure.

**On slide:** Two columns only:

| Original | Dyslexia mode |
|----------|---------------|
| "Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants use sunlight to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen." | "Plants make food using sunlight. / This is called photosynthesis. / Leaves use chlorophyll to catch light." |

**Speaker notes:**  
"Notice we didn't just swap words. We changed how the information is packaged — chunk size, emphasis, sequence. That's what makes this useful in a real classroom."

### Slide 5 — How it works

**Headline:** Three steps. No reformatting.

**On slide:**
1. **Upload or paste** — worksheet, PDF, notes, assignment  
2. **Choose a mode** — dyslexia, ADHD, APD, or autism structure  
3. **Share** — compare side by side, download PDF, translate

**Visual:** Three numbered steps with icons. Match the site's "How it works" section.

**Speaker notes:**  
"No LMS integration required for the demo. Teacher drops in what they already have, picks how the student needs to experience it, and gets a shareable version. We also support translation — Spanish, Hindi, French, simplified English — on top of the accessibility mode."

---

### Slide 6 — Why now / differentiation

**Headline:** AI that changes structure — with a demo that never breaks.

**On slide:**
- **Structural transforms**, not cosmetic paraphrasing  
- **Gemini-powered** with automatic local fallback (quota-safe demos)  
- **Teacher-first** — works with existing materials, not a new curriculum

**Visual:** Simple 2×2 or three checkmarks. No competitor bashing.

**Speaker notes:**  
"Lots of tools summarize or simplify. AdaptED changes document architecture per learning need. We use Gemini for quality, but built a local fallback engine so demos never die when API quota runs out — important for classrooms and for today."

## Design checklist (visual polish)

1. **Dark background** — match the site (feels premium, on-brand)  
2. **One accent color** — teal/indigo from AdaptED, not rainbow slides  
3. **Huge headlines** — 44pt+; body 24pt max  
4. **Screenshots &gt; stock photos** — the product is your best visual asset  
5. **Animate the demo, not the deck** — slide transitions should be boring; the live mode switch should be exciting  
6. **Consistent mode colors** — Dyslexia, ADHD, APD, Autism use the same hues as the website  
7. **QR code on title and closing slides** — judges scan during your talk

**Tools that work well:** Google Slides, Canva, Pitch.com, or Keynote. Export PDF as backup.

## What to cut if you're over time

1. Drop "How it works" — demo already shows it  
2. Drop "Why now" — fold one line into the problem slide  
3. Never drop the live demo or side-by-side proof slide
